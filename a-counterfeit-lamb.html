A Counterfeit Lamb

The false prophet looks like a lamb and speaks like the dragon: what each reading points to, and whether it shows a voice that deceives or only a quiet manner.

The mark
A second beast rises from the earth with two horns like a lamb's but speaks like a dragon; it wields the first beast's authority in its sight and makes the earth worship the first beast (Revelation 13:11–12; 16:13; 19:20; Matthew 7:15).
What the Church teaches
The final trial will take the form of "a religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth" (CCC 675). The false prophet is that deception given a voice: power dressed as meekness, and the dragon's purposes spoken as if they were good. Church teaching

Mark 4 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

Read in context (Standard 4), the mark has three parts: a lamb-like appearance, a dragon's voice (deception in the service of the dragon), and authority borrowed from the first beast and spent on turning the world to it. Revelation calls this figure the false prophet, a religious deceiver who works signs (19:20). A gentle manner or a role as someone's spokesman is not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show words that deceive in the dragon's service, and it must keep any person's faith or people out of the case entirely (Standard 28; Nostra Aetate 4).

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John's case fits the shape of the mark better than most: a quiet man with a peacemaker's record, holding no office of his own, who acts with the president's authority in the president's sight. His strongest evidence for the dragon's voice is the 2024 remark that Gaza's people could be moved out so its waterfront could be cleared and built, which speaks of people as an obstacle to property. But the mark's center is religious deception, and nothing yet shows him in a religious role or promoting worship; his peace work has brought real goods, which the reading must weigh honestly rather than treat as masks. What the evidence shows is a lamb's appearance and borrowed authority, not yet the false prophet's voice. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as the Antichrist's armor-bearer. Thiel's reading does not take up this mark.

Arminjon does not distinguish the second beast, so he offers nothing to weigh on this mark.

Miceli distinguishes the two beasts as the text does, and warns against confident identifications of the second: he counts the claim that an antipope will be the false prophet as speculation "presented as historical facts" (p. 16). Fr. John's grading of this mark as probable, not settled, meets that warning in form; the evidence must still carry it.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
