Mark 11 weighed

Control of Buying and Selling

A stamp on hand or forehead, bearing the beast's name or number, without which no one can buy or sell: what each reading points to, and whether it shows the mark or only the tools.

The mark
The second beast forces all, small and great, to receive a stamp on the right hand or forehead, so that no one can buy or sell without the beast's name or number; accepting it is joined to worship (Revelation 13:16–17; 14:9–11; 20:4).
Authority
Scripture
In depth
Mark 11 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

Read in context (Standard 4), the mark is universal, forced, tied to buying and selling, bears the beast's name or number, and is joined to worship (14:9). A technology of identity or payment is not the mark by itself; it becomes the mark only when it is required and joined to the worship of the beast. A reading that claims the mark must say how close any system comes to those conditions, and must not frighten readers by calling every new technology the mark (Standard 24; Standard 27).

Every reading should also keep in view what the mark counterfeits. St. Peter Chrysologus, Doctor of the Church, says of creation that “the second Adam stamped his image on the first Adam when he created him,” and he asks the reborn to “put on the complete image of our Creator… in innocence, simplicity, gentleness, patience, humility, mercy, harmony” (Sermon 117; Office of Readings, Saturday of the Twenty-ninth Week in Ordinary Time) A Father or Doctor. The beast's stamp is pressed over an image God put there first, and the best preparation against it is to bear that image more fully.

Fr. Vincent Miceli, S.J. (1981) Partly The second beast brands men "on the forehead or on the right hand with the mark of the Prince of Darkness" (The Antichrist, p. 37); reporting Adso, those who follow him "will be marked on the forehead with a sign" (p. 87) In ch. 10 he makes the mark inward: Catholics who deny Christ's divinity already "bear the characteristic mark of the Antichrist" (p. 194). In his own chapters he never takes up buying and selling or identifies the mark with anything. His appendix reprints, without comment, a 1975 report of a Brussels computer "named 'The Beast'" and a number "laser-tattooed" on hand or forehead, without which "No member could buy or sell" (p. 280), a story long since shown to be a hoax 9 met
Peter Thiel Partly His world state would control science and technology for "peace and safety"; he is himself a co-founder of PayPal, a payment system, and of Palantir, a data-analysis company used by governments He does not read a mark of buying and selling, and he defends rather than warns against the technologies others see in it —
Fr. John Yes, conditions not met He holds his own test: the mark is recognized when one cannot buy or sell without it and must worship to receive it, and neither is met. Three lines: (1) Tools for Humanity's World, founded by Sam Altman, which reads each person's iris to issue a World ID, now held by more than 18 million people in over 160 countries, launched in the U.S. in 2025, tied to a Visa card for the scanned, a dollar stablecoin, Tinder, Zoom and a 2026 suite for banks, and suspended or investigated by more than a dozen nations; (2) the first beast's name on the means of buying and selling: his signature on currency, his face on coins, and his family's stablecoin; (3) the second beast among the builders: Altman beside the president at Stargate, Kushner's AI company built on OpenAI and backed by payment-company founders, with no direct tie to Tools for Humanity found (his case) World ID is voluntary and no system is required to buy or sell; none requires worship; Tools for Humanity is not Kushner's company, and links through Altman and shared investors are association, not evidence; many nations resist it; a name on money and a family crypto business are conflicts of interest, not the mark 7 met; 9 met: he says neither condition is met; 24 met: no technology is called the mark; 4 not met as yet; 30: graded probable, not yet seen

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John's case on this mark is careful: he states his own test, says plainly that neither of its conditions is met, and calls nothing the mark. The evidence he gathers is real and well documented: an identity system that reads the human eye, spreading into payments, banking and daily life, and resisted by many nations; the first beast's name moving onto money; and the second beast near the builders of AI and payments. As a watch on how the mark could become possible, it is sound and in keeping with the Church's warnings about technology. As an argument that the mark is present, or that the man he names is building it, it does not yet reach: the systems are voluntary, no worship is joined to them, and no direct link between Kushner and Tools for Humanity has been found. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully of the end. Thiel's reading turns from the danger the others see in these tools.

Arminjon reads the mark as a sign of apostasy that shuts its refusers out of trade, close to Fr. John's own test, and names no object.

Miceli never takes up buying and selling in his own voice; the one report of it he prints, in his appendix, was a hoax, which is a caution for every reading that looks for the mark in a technology.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
