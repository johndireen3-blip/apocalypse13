Mark 5 weighed

Boasting and Blasphemy

A mouth that boasts of itself and then speaks against God: what each reading points to, and whether it reaches the second half of the mark.

The mark
The beast is given a mouth uttering proud boasts and blasphemies, against God, his name, his dwelling and those who dwell in heaven (Revelation 13:1, 5–6; Daniel 7:8, 25; 2 Thessalonians 2:4).
What the Church teaches
Blasphemy is uttering against God, inwardly or outwardly, words of hatred, reproach or defiance; it extends to words against the Church, the saints and sacred things (CCC 2148). The Catechism names the final deception as a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh (CCC 675). The beast's mouth is that self-glorification spoken aloud. Church teaching

Mark 5 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

The mark has two halves, and read in context (Standard 4) they belong together: proud boasts, and blasphemy against God and what is his (Revelation 13:5–6). Boasting of oneself is common among rulers and is not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show the second half: words of reproach or defiance against God, his name, his dwelling or his saints (CCC 2148), or self-exaltation into God's place (2 Thessalonians 2:4; CCC 675).

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John's evidence for the first half of the mark, proud boasts, is extensive and documented. His evidence for the second half rests on four episodes that each touch sacred things. The strongest is the April 2026 image of himself robed as Christ the healer, posted while he was attacking the pope over the war with Iran; it was widely judged blasphemous, including by his own supporters, but he disclaimed it as a doctor and took it down. Taken together, the episodes show a pattern of setting himself in sacred roles and treating the Church lightly; none is a plain word spoken against God, which leaves the second half of the mark argued rather than shown. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as the claim to divine honor that ends the beast's career. Thiel's reading does not take up this mark.

Arminjon reads the mark as St. Irenaeus does, as blasphemy against God himself, with no application to test.

Miceli reads the mark as hostility to God himself, with no application to test.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
