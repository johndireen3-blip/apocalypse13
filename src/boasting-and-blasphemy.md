## What the mark requires

The mark has two halves, and read in context ([Standard 4](/the-standards#s4)) they belong together: proud boasts, and blasphemy against God and what is his (Revelation 13:5–6). Boasting of oneself is common among rulers and is not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show the second half: words of reproach or defiance against God, his name, his dwelling or his saints (CCC 2148), or self-exaltation into God's place (2 Thessalonians 2:4; CCC 675).

## Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John's evidence for the first half of the mark, proud boasts, is extensive and documented. His evidence for the second half rests on four episodes that each touch sacred things. The strongest is the April 2026 image of himself robed as Christ the healer, posted while he was attacking the pope over the war with Iran; it was widely judged blasphemous, including by his own supporters, but he disclaimed it as a doctor and took it down. Taken together, the episodes show a pattern of setting himself in sacred roles and treating the Church lightly; none is a plain word spoken against God, which leaves the second half of the mark argued rather than shown. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as the claim to divine honor that ends the beast's career. Thiel's reading does not take up this mark.

Arminjon reads the mark as St. Irenaeus does, as blasphemy against God himself, with no application to test.

Miceli reads the mark as hostility to God himself, with no application to test.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
