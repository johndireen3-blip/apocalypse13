## Beyond Revelation 13 {#beyond}

The Fathers did not read Revelation 13 alone. They read it with Daniel 7 and 12, 2 Thessalonians 2 and Revelation 11, and most readings stand or fall on these texts as much as on the marks. This table records where each reading stands on them. It reports positions only; the weighing will follow on pages of their own.

Arminjon is cited from *The End of the Present World and the Mysteries of the Future Life* (Sophia Institute Press, 2008), First and Second Conferences; Miceli from *The Antichrist* (Roman Catholic Books, 1981), chapters 1–4 so far, so "Not in chs. 1–4" means only that those chapters do not take it up; Irenaeus from *Against Heresies*, Book V.

**On row I.** Irenaeus and Arminjon report an opinion held by many Fathers, that the Antichrist would be born a Jew; Miceli reports it from Irenaeus and Cyril without endorsing it in his own voice. It is recorded here as part of the history of interpretation, and it is weighed by [Standard 28](/the-standards#s28): the Church teaches that what happened in Christ's Passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today" (*Nostra Aetate* 4), and she awaits Israel's recognition of Christ in hope (CCC 674). Arminjon's own pages on the Jews of his day (pp. 60–63) honor them as the people who "has given to the world Christ, the Apostles, and the Immaculate Virgin," but also cast them as a people bent on gold and power. That part of his reading does not meet Standard 28. Miceli, too, in his own voice, writes of "that race which rejected Christ as its king and Messiah" (p. 56) and of "the willingness of the Jewish leaders to be seduced by the Antichrist" (p. 54), which charges a people collectively, as *Nostra Aetate* 4 forbids; those lines do not meet Standard 28 either.
