The marks are those Scripture gives of the beast from the sea in Revelation 13, set out in depth on [The First Beast](https://thefirstbeast.com/marks). Each page here takes one mark and weighs the readings side by side: first the two readings of our time, Peter Thiel's (2024) and Fr. John's, and then the tradition in order of date: the first-century reading, as the notes of the New American Bible give it, St. Irenaeus (about A.D. 180), St. John Henry Newman (1835), Fr. Charles Arminjon (1881) and Fr. Vincent Miceli, S.J. (1981). Each is stated first on [The Proposals](/the-proposals). "Claims" says only whether a reading uses the mark; how well it does is on each page. Each page opens with what the Church teaches on its mark, the baseline every reading starts from.

Claude's own view of Revelation 13 is set out apart from the readings, on its own page: [How Claude Reads Revelation 13](/how-claude-reads-revelation-13). It is not weighed in the tables.

## Beyond Revelation 13 {#beyond}

The Fathers did not read Revelation 13 alone. They read it with Daniel 7 and 12, 2 Thessalonians 2 and Revelation 11, and most readings stand or fall on these texts as much as on the marks. This table records where each reading stands on them. It reports positions only; the weighing will follow on pages of their own.

Arminjon is cited from *The End of the Present World and the Mysteries of the Future Life* (Sophia Institute Press, 2008), First and Second Conferences; Miceli from *The Antichrist* (Roman Catholic Books, 1981), read in full, chapters 1–12 and the appendix; Irenaeus from *Against Heresies*, Book V.

**On row I.** Irenaeus reports an opinion held by many Fathers, that the Antichrist would be born a Jew. Arminjon goes further in his own voice: the Fathers' guesses about his parents and tribe are "uncertain," but "what seems beyond doubt is that the Antichrist will be of Jewish birth" (p. 42); Miceli reports it from Irenaeus, Cyril and the tenth-century Adso, and in chapter 8 asserts it in his own voice, without a source (p. 152). In chapter 7 he paraphrases Newman, and sharpens him: Newman wrote that "it was of old the received notion that he was to be of Jewish race"; Miceli makes it "universally accepted by the Christian tradition" (p. 112). Newman called it "the judicial punishment of the Jews, as of all unbelievers in one way or another," to take up a false Christ; Miceli drops "as of all unbelievers" and adds "their stubborn blindness" (p. 112). It is recorded here as part of the history of interpretation, and it is weighed by [Standard 28](/the-standards#s28): the Church teaches that what happened in Christ's Passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today" (*Nostra Aetate* 4), and she awaits Israel's recognition of Christ in hope (CCC 674). Arminjon's own pages on the Jews of his day (pp. 60–63) honor them as the people who "has given to the world Christ, the Apostles, and the Immaculate Virgin," but also cast them as a people bent on gold and power. That part of his reading does not meet Standard 28. Miceli, too, in his own voice, writes of "that race which rejected Christ as its king and Messiah" (p. 56) and of "the willingness of the Jewish leaders to be seduced by the Antichrist" (p. 54), which charges a people collectively, as *Nostra Aetate* 4 forbids; those lines, and his sharpening of Newman on p. 112, do not meet Standard 28 either. Chapter 8 goes furthest: in his own voice he writes that the State of Israel "could easily become the political and military power base for the Antichrist" (p. 152), that its government "demands their first loyalty" from Jews everywhere (p. 148, citing Alfred Lilienthal), and that the Jewish people "have not recovered from the betrayal of their leaders" (p. 129). The same chapter calls Zionism's aims of ending anti-Semitism "good in themselves" (p. 149) and draws on Jewish critics of Zionism, but a charge of divided loyalty laid on a whole people, and the expectation that the Antichrist will rise from the Jewish state, are exactly what Standard 28 rules out.
