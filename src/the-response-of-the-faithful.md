## What the mark requires

This mark is not claimed; it is lived. Revelation 13 is written so that the holy ones will hear, endure and keep faith, and will not answer the beast with its own weapons (13:10; Matthew 26:52). So every reading is weighed here by its fruit ([Standard 12](/the-standards#s12)) and by whether it builds up charity ([Standard 27](/the-standards#s27)): what does it ask of its readers, and does it leave them more faithful, more patient and more loving, or more fearful, curious and hostile?

## Verdict on the arguments

On this mark Fr. John's reading asks of its readers what Revelation 13:9–10 asks: hearing, endurance, faith, refusal of violence and love for those on every side, and it says plainly that the reading fails if it produces fear or hostility. That is the right test, and he sets it for himself. Whether his reading meets it cannot be shown on a page; it will be shown in its readers. St. Irenaeus remains the model: a reading of the last enemy written to make the Church patient and hopeful. Thiel's reading asks for vigilance against fear, but its response is political, and its naming of living people as figures of the Antichrist's program does not build up charity.

Arminjon asks for prayer, charity and faith now; his pages on the Jews of his own day are weighed under Standard 28, as noted on [Beyond Revelation 13](/marks#beyond).

Miceli asks for a "holy Resistance Movement" against the devil, joined to watchfulness and prayer; two lines of his fourth chapter on the Jewish people are weighed under Standard 28, as noted on [Beyond Revelation 13](/marks#beyond).

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
