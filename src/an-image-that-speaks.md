## What the mark requires

Read in context ([Standard 4](/the-standards#s4)), the mark has four parts: an image made for the first beast at the false prophet's urging, breath given to it, a voice, and worship enforced on pain of death. The Church teaches that substituting a made thing for God is idolatry (CCC 2113; *Antiqua et Nova* 105), but she does not teach that artificial intelligence is the image of Revelation 13, and she calls for it to be governed, not condemned (*Magnifica Humanitas*). A reading that claims the mark must show an image made for the one it names and honored as the text describes, not only that speaking machines exist.

## Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John holds this mark open, which is the honest place for it, and he joins two documented threads: machine-made images of the man he reads as the first beast, set before the world, and the man he reads as the second beast building AI for governments. As a watch on where the mark could be fulfilled, that is reasonable, and it agrees with the Church's warnings about idolatry and technology. As an argument that the mark is now fulfilled, it falls short: no image has been made at the false prophet's urging, given a voice, and honored by command. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully of the end. Thiel's reading turns the warning around, treating restraint of technology, not its idolization, as the Antichrist's work.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
