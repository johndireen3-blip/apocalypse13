## What the mark requires

Read in context ([Standard 4](/the-standards#s4)), the holy ones are "those who keep God's commandments and bear witness to Jesus" (Revelation 12:17), and the war is against them as such: it ends in their death for refusing to worship (Revelation 13:15). Conflict between the Church and a government over policy is common in history and is not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show the faithful opposed or penalized for their faith or witness, and the war moving toward the conquest the text describes. Because the Church teaches that the final trial will "shake the faith of many believers" (CCC 675), a reading must also be careful not to call every hardship persecution ([Standard 9](/the-standards#s9)).

## Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John documents a real and sustained conflict between the Church and the government over the treatment of migrants, in which clergy, a religious sister and parishioners have been touched by enforcement, the bishops have spoken with unusual unity, and the Pope has intervened. His argument that the Church's ministry to the stranger is part of her witness to Jesus is sound Catholic teaching (Matthew 25:35; CCC 2241). But the conflict is over immigration policy, not over the faith as such; those affected were not penalized for being Christians; and the courts have repeatedly protected the Church. What his evidence shows is pressure on the Church's witness, not yet the war and conquest the text describes. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as a final persecution still to come. Thiel's reading does not take up this mark.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
