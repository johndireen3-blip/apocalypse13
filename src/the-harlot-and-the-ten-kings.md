## What the chapter requires

Revelation 17 offers its own interpretation (17:7–18), and the Church's first reading of it is Rome (NABRE notes). A reading for our time must fit the details the angel gives, not only the images: the harlot is a *city* ruling kings and enriching merchants, drunk on the blood of the saints; the kings are uncrowned, brief, of one mind, and turn on her at the end ([Standard 4](/the-standards#s4); [Standard 15](/the-standards#s15)). Because this chapter is "the graveyard of confident readings," humility is the standard most tested here ([Standard 9](/the-standards#s9); [Standard 23](/the-standards#s23)). The seven heads (17:9–11) are weighed under [mark 2](/built-from-daniels-beasts).

## Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John approaches Revelation 17 with more reserve than chapter 13, grading only the harlot and holding the ten kings, the three and the burning open. His reading of the harlot as a world economy of wealth and technology fits several of the angel's details well: sovereignty over kings, merchants grown rich, nations led astray, and a rider carried by a greater power. On the most serious detail, the blood of the holy ones, he widens the holy ones to all people of faith, with support from 18:24 and the Church's teaching on those who seek God (CCC 841–843); on that reading Myanmar answers the detail, though a platform's part in violence it did not intend is weaker than a city that kills the saints. His reading of a "city" remains an analogy. His treatment of the ten kings and the three is honest about its failures. St. Irenaeus's reading remains the anchor for the kings, with Augustine's caution beside it. Thiel's reading is the most striking contrast in the whole comparison: it would call the burning of the harlot the Antichrist's work.

Arminjon reads the ten kings as the Antichrist's lieutenants and does not take up the harlot.

Miceli reads the city as pagan Rome and the ten kings as types of the powers that will persecute the Church, leaving their last fulfillment unstated.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
