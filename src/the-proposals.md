## The first-century reading: Rome and its emperors [[auth:theologian]]

Most Scripture scholars today read Revelation first of all as a book written for Christians under the Roman Empire, and the notes of the New American Bible, Revised Edition, the translation read at Mass in the United States, give that reading. "The Book of Revelation cannot be adequately understood except against the historical background that occasioned its writing," which was "ruthless persecution of the early church by the Roman authorities" ([Introduction](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/0)). The beast from the sea "symbolizes the Roman empire; the seven heads represent the emperors." The healed wound recalls the legend that Nero would return. The worship of the beast is "emperor worship, which Domitian insisted upon and ruthlessly enforced." The number 666 is most likely "the emperor Caesar Nero." Babylon is pagan Rome, "the city on seven hills" ([notes on chapter 13](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/13); [chapter 17](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/revelation/17)).

This reading does not say that Revelation is only about the past. The same introduction says the book "remains valid and meaningful for Christians of all time," and that its perspective reaches to "the end of the present age when Christ will come in glory." The Church teaches that a final trial and the Antichrist are still to come (CCC 675–677), so a reading that stopped at the first century would be incomplete; the notes do not stop there, though they say little about what lies ahead.

It is weighed here because it is the main alternative to every reading on this site that looks to the future, Fr. John's included. If each mark already had a sufficient meaning in John's own day, then any later application is a *further* fulfillment, and must say what it adds ([Standard 15](/the-standards#s15)). The notes are those of the NABRE as published by the United States Conference of Catholic Bishops; they are a work of scholarship, not of the Magisterium.

## St. Irenaeus: the classic reading [[auth:father]]

St. Irenaeus of Lyon, writing around A.D. 180, gives the earliest full reading of the beast by a Father of the Church (*Against Heresies* V.25–30). He reads the beast from the sea as one man to come at the end, who receives all the power of the devil, sets himself up to be worshiped as God, and rises among ten kings into whom the Roman Empire is divided. He reads the beast from the earth as his armor-bearer, the false prophet. He will not guess the beast's name: the number, he says, was left hidden so that it would be recognized only when it is fulfilled (V.30.3).

He is weighed here as the classic reading because he applies the text to no one in the present, so his reading shows what a faithful reading of the marks looks like before any application is made. St. Hippolytus and St. Cyril of Jerusalem are drawn in where they add to him.

## St. John Henry Newman: the Fathers gathered [[auth:father]]

St. John Henry Newman (1801–1890) preached four Advent sermons on the Antichrist at Oxford in 1835, while still an Anglican clergyman, and published them as "The Patristical Idea of Antichrist." As a Catholic he kept them in print in his collected works. He was canonized in 2019 and declared a Doctor of the Church by Pope Leo XIV in 2025. His aim was not to offer a reading of his own but to set out what the Fathers expected, while noting that they are surer guides in doctrine than in unfulfilled prophecy.

With the Fathers he holds that the Antichrist will be "one man, an individual, not a power or a kingdom" ([lecture 1](https://newmanreader.org/works/arguments/antichrist/lecture1.html)); that he comes after a great apostasy and when the power that restrains him, the Roman Empire and the order that descends from it, is taken away; that he will be an open blasphemer who nevertheless sets up a worship of his own ([lecture 2](https://newmanreader.org/works/arguments/antichrist/lecture2.html)); that the harlot of Revelation 17 is the city of Rome ([lecture 3](https://newmanreader.org/works/arguments/antichrist/lecture3.html)); and that his persecution will last three and a half years and silence the Church's public worship ([lecture 4](https://newmanreader.org/works/arguments/antichrist/lecture4.html)). He names the shadows that have gone before, Antiochus, Julian, Mahomet and the French Revolution, and he names no one as the Antichrist. Of his own age he says only that an apostasy seemed to be gathering, and that whether it would bring the Antichrist "we cannot know."

He is weighed here as the fullest gathering of the Fathers by a Doctor of the Church, and because Miceli builds on him. The lectures are cited by number and section from the text at newmanreader.org.

## Fr. Charles Arminjon: the tradition gathered [[auth:theologian]]

Fr. Charles Arminjon (1824–1885), a priest of Chambéry in Savoy, was ordained in 1849 as a Jesuit, left the Society with its approval in 1859 to teach at the major seminary of Chambéry, and was named an apostolic missionary by Rome in 1863, after which he gave himself wholly to preaching. He preached nine conferences at the cathedral of Chambéry, published in 1881 as *The End of the Present World and the Mysteries of the Future Life*. St. Thérèse of Lisieux read it in 1887, at fourteen, and wrote that reading it "was one of the greatest graces of my life." In the Second Conference he gathers the Fathers and Doctors, above all Augustine, Jerome, Thomas, Bellarmine and Suárez, into one account. The Antichrist will be one man, possessed by Satan from his youth. He will demand worship as God, work false wonders, persecute the Church across the whole earth for forty-two months, and be struck down by Christ.

He reads one beast only, and gives the second beast's signs to the Antichrist himself. Like Irenaeus he names no one and will not decode 666. He warned that present calamities cannot prove the end is near (p. 13), yet from the signs of his own century he judged the Antichrist's nearness "more probable" than his remoteness, adding that centuries are little "compared with the years of eternity" (p. 21).

He is weighed here as the tradition's synthesis on the eve of the twentieth century, and because Fr. John reads with him. Page numbers are to the English translation by Susan Conroy and Peter McEnerny (Sophia Institute Press, 2008).

## Fr. Vincent Miceli, S.J.: theology, not theology-fiction [[auth:theologian]]

Fr. Vincent P. Miceli, a Jesuit priest, wrote *The Antichrist* (1981, with the *nihil obstat* and *imprimatur* of the Archdiocese of New York) "in order to prevent the faithful from falling into erroneous predictions" by setting out the Church's teaching afresh (p. 16). With "all the Fathers and theologians" he holds that the Antichrist will be one individual person (p. 18), preceded by many lesser antichrists, his "types" and "precursors" (p. 33). He distinguishes the two beasts as the text does: the beast from the earth is the false prophet, the first beast's assistant (pp. 37–38).

His chief warning is against "theology-fiction": "the presentation of personal speculations as *facts* found in these prophecies" (p. 15). His rule is that "the intelligent and clear must be presented with discernment; the mysterious left for explanation by the events" (p. 21). He names no one, sets no date, and reads the irreligion of his own age as a warning, not a fulfillment.

He is weighed here as a modern Catholic theologian writing against the speculation of his day. Surveying the Middle Ages, he judges that holy men who believed the Antichrist had already come "allowed themselves to be deceived" (p. 85), and that "all chiliastic movements outlive the nonfulfillment of their prophecies" (p. 97). In chapter 8, "Modern Shadows," he names many modern figures and regimes as "types, precursors, shadows" of the Antichrist (p. 147), from Marx and Freud to Hitler and Stalin, and even Roosevelt and Churchill at Yalta and the Kennedy administration over the 1963 coup in South Vietnam (pp. 140–146), though he calls no one the Antichrist himself. The same chapter expects the Antichrist to rule from Jerusalem, with the State of Israel as his "power base" (p. 152), which this site weighs under Standard 28. Chapter 9 turns to "antichrists" within the Church, naming dissenting Catholic theologians and, without naming them, two Jesuit university presidents as "mini-antichrists" and "forerunners" (pp. 165–182), though he casts no one as the false prophet. In chapter 10 he gives, at last, his own view of the restrainer: "the strong Faith of the Church is what has restrained the coming of the Antichrist so far" (p. 222), and judges that "the portents of his nearness are evident everywhere," though "his name and his time are known only to God" (p. 219). In his last chapter he turns to Mary, the Woman of Revelation 12, whom he expects to "destroy" the Antichrist (p. 258). The book has been read in full; chapters 4–7 mostly report the Fathers, the medieval writers and Newman.

## Peter Thiel: the world state [[auth:theologian]]

Peter Thiel, the technology investor and co-founder of PayPal and Palantir, is not a Catholic, but he has lectured widely on the Antichrist since 2024, including in Rome. His thesis, as he set it out on the record ([Hoover Institution, *Uncommon Knowledge*, 2024](https://www.hoover.org/research/part-ii-apocalypse-now-peter-thiel-ancient-prophecies-and-modern-tech)): the Antichrist is a type that can be a person, a system or an ideology. In our age it will come to power by stirring fear of catastrophe (nuclear war, pandemics, artificial intelligence, climate) and offering "peace and safety" (1 Thessalonians 5:3) through a world government that halts science and technology. He sees runaway technology as the danger of Armageddon and a stifling world state as the danger of the Antichrist, and judges the second more likely.

He has since published two essays in *First Things* with Sam Wolfe. ["Voyages to the End of the World"](https://firstthings.com/voyages-to-the-end-of-the-world/) (November 2025) traces the Antichrist through literature, from Bacon's *New Atlantis* and Swift's *Gulliver's Travels* to the graphic novel *Watchmen* and the manga *One Piece*: in each he finds a ruler who offers the world peace through science and a single government. To the question "Antichrist or Armageddon?" the essay answers, "Neither." ["The Pope and the Antichrist"](https://firstthings.com/the-pope-and-the-antichrist/) (August 2026), which grew from his lectures in Rome, argues that Benedict XVI "believed he was living in the end times," and draws on Joachim of Fiore, St. Bonaventure and Soloviev's tale of an Antichrist who says, "I shall bring peace." He names no living person as the Antichrist in either essay, and writes, "It is not my place to tell the Church what time it is."

In four private lectures in 2025, known through press reports, he was sharper: "in the 21st century, the Antichrist is a Luddite who wants to stop all science"; he called named critics of technology "the legionnaires of the Antichrist," read Revelation 13:16–17 as a world financial system, and called the American empire "the natural candidate for Katechon," the restrainer of 2 Thessalonians 2, and also a possible Antichrist ([Reason](https://reason.com/2025/10/14/i-listened-to-over-7-hours-of-peter-thiels-leaked-antichrist-lectures-theyre-surprisingly-libertarian/)).

Catholic voices have answered him. Fr. Paolo Benanti, who has advised two popes on technology, called him "a political theologian operating at the very heart of the Silicon Valley ecosystem," and *Avvenire*, the Italian bishops' newspaper, wrote that his definition could apply to "anyone who places limits on unlimited progress" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/16/peter-thiel-antichrist-theory-rome-lectures-vatican-response/)). The theologian Douglas Farrow asks of the two authors, "Why do they show no interest in Jesus Christ?" ([Farrow](https://douglasfarrow.substack.com/p/corrupting-the-katechon)).

His published words are weighed here first. His private lectures are cited only as reported in the press and are marked as such. His reading has a page of its own, with a timeline of where he has spoken and how it fares mark by mark: [Peter Thiel and the Antichrist](/peter-thiel-and-the-antichrist).

</div>

## Fr. John: the American presidency [[auth:proposal]]

Fr. John, a Catholic diocesan priest who founded and directs The Ephesus Challenge, reads the seven heads of the beast as seven world powers, counted from John's day, with the United States as the seventh. He proposes that the first beast of Revelation 13 is Donald J. Trump: the seventh head his first term, the eighth his return to power in the 47th presidency. He grades each mark *possible* or *probable*, never settled, and sets out the evidence against his reading beside the evidence for it. The Church has not taught this reading, and he does not say she has.

He makes his case on [his own site](https://apocalypse12.org/): mark by mark for [the first beast](https://apocalypse12.org/the-first-beast) and [the second](https://apocalypse12.org/the-second-beast), with how he reads the chapter set out on [Watch](https://apocalypse12.org/watch). Here it is weighed by the same standards as every other reading, and he asks that it be tested.
