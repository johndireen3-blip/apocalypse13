A reading of Revelation 13 is weighed here in two ways. First, against the **marks**: what Scripture and the Church reveal about the beasts, set out in depth on [The First Beast](https://thefirstbeast.com/marks). Second, against these **standards**: how a reading uses the marks. The first three are the Church's own criteria for interpreting Scripture (CCC 112–114); the rest come from the Church's documents, the Fathers, Scripture's own tests, and the charter of The Ephesus Challenge.

A standard is a question asked of an argument. Meeting it does not make a reading true, and failing one does not make it false; it shows where the reading is strong and where it needs more support. Every proposal is asked the same questions, Fr. John's included.

## The first twelve

<tr><th>#</th><th>Standard</th><th>The question it asks of a proposal</th><th>Basis</th></tr>
<tr id="s1"><td><strong>1</strong></td><td><strong>Unity of Scripture</strong></td><td>Does it read the passage within the whole of Scripture, not in isolation?</td><td>CCC 112; <em>Dei Verbum</em> 12</td></tr>
<tr id="s2"><td><strong>2</strong></td><td><strong>Living Tradition</strong></td><td>Does it engage how the Fathers and the Church have read the passage?</td><td>CCC 113</td></tr>
<tr id="s3"><td><strong>3</strong></td><td><strong>Analogy of faith</strong></td><td>Is it consistent with the truths of the faith as a whole?</td><td>CCC 114</td></tr>
<tr id="s4"><td><strong>4</strong></td><td><strong>Text in its context</strong></td><td>Does each verse it uses say what the proposal needs, in its own context? For example, 1 Thess 5:3 concerns the sudden Day of the Lord.</td><td>CCC 109–110</td></tr>
<tr id="s5"><td><strong>5</strong></td><td><strong>No date-setting</strong></td><td>Does it avoid fixing the day or the hour?</td><td>Matt 24:36; CCC 673</td></tr>
<tr id="s6"><td><strong>6</strong></td><td><strong>Private revelation in its place</strong></td><td>Does it treat apparitions and private revelations as aids, never as additions to the faith?</td><td>CCC 67</td></tr>
<tr id="s7"><td><strong>7</strong></td><td><strong>Checkable evidence</strong></td><td>Does each mark it claims rest on evidence a reader can verify?</td><td>Charter, rule 5</td></tr>
<tr id="s8"><td><strong>8</strong></td><td><strong>Consistency</strong></td><td>Has it held steady, or shifted with circumstances or politics?</td><td>Charter safeguard</td></tr>
<tr id="s9"><td><strong>9</strong></td><td><strong>Humility</strong></td><td>Does it claim no more than its evidence allows?</td><td>2 Pet 1:20; Augustine's reserve on 2 Thess 2 (<em>City of God</em> XX.19)</td></tr>
<tr id="s10"><td><strong>10</strong></td><td><strong>Charity and justice</strong></td><td>Does it treat persons justly and avoid stirring hostility?</td><td>Charter, rules 1 and 4</td></tr>
<tr id="s11"><td><strong>11</strong></td><td><strong>Agenda or sincere search</strong></td><td>Is its method sound? Relevant context is stated with sources; any judgment of motive is marked as Fr. John's assessment.</td><td>Charter safeguard</td></tr>
<tr id="s12"><td><strong>12</strong></td><td><strong>Fruit</strong></td><td>Does it lead readers to endurance, faith and hope rather than fear?</td><td>Rev 13:10; Luke 12:7; charter, rule 6</td></tr>

## Further standards

Added on October 20, 2026, from the Church's documents on reading Scripture, the Fathers, and Scripture's own tests.

<tr id="s13"><td><strong>13</strong></td><td><strong>Literal sense first</strong></td><td>Does it establish what the text says before building a spiritual or future reading on it, and argue only from the literal sense?</td><td>CCC 116; Aquinas, <em>ST</em> I q. 1 a. 10 ad 1; <em>Divino Afflante Spiritu</em> 23; <em>Providentissimus Deus</em> 15</td></tr>
<tr id="s14"><td><strong>14</strong></td><td><strong>Literary form and the writer's intention</strong></td><td>Does it read apocalyptic images as apocalyptic, asking what the sacred writer meant in his time?</td><td><em>Dei Verbum</em> 12; CCC 109–110; <em>Divino Afflante Spiritu</em> 33–38</td></tr>
<tr id="s15"><td><strong>15</strong></td><td><strong>First fulfillment, then further fulfillment</strong></td><td>Does it account for what the text meant first (Antiochus, Rome) before applying it to the end?</td><td><em>Dei Verbum</em> 12; <em>Verbum Domini</em> 40–41</td></tr>

<tr id="s19"><td><strong>19</strong></td><td><strong>Levels of authority kept distinct</strong></td><td>Does it label each claim as Scripture, Church teaching, common opinion of the Fathers, a theologian's view, private revelation or the proposer's own view?</td><td><em>Dei Verbum</em> 10; CCC 67; charter, rule 2</td></tr>
<tr id="s20"><td><strong>20</strong></td><td><strong>Application without manipulation</strong></td><td>When it applies the text to present events, does it rest on sound exegesis and avoid projecting ideologies onto the text?</td><td>PBC, <em>Interpretation of the Bible in the Church</em> (1993) I.F, IV.A.3; <em>Verbum Domini</em> 44</td></tr>

<tr id="s22"><td><strong>22</strong></td><td><strong>Test everything</strong></td><td>Has it been tested against Christ come in the flesh, and does it hold what is good?</td><td>1 Thess 5:19–21; 1 John 4:1–3</td></tr>
<tr id="s23"><td><strong>23</strong></td><td><strong>Failed predictions count</strong></td><td>Has it, or a version of it, predicted things that did not happen?</td><td>Deut 18:21–22</td></tr>
<tr id="s24"><td><strong>24</strong></td><td><strong>No unhealthy curiosity</strong></td><td>Does it seek to know the future in a way that replaces trust in Providence?</td><td>CCC 2115–2116; Arminjon, <em>The End of the Present World</em>, p. 14: reckonings of the end are "idle quests, lacking any purpose other than the satisfaction of vain curiosity"</td></tr>
<tr id="s25"><td><strong>25</strong></td><td><strong>No fixed time for the Antichrist</strong></td><td>Does it avoid announcing when the Antichrist will come?</td><td>Lateran V, Session 11 (1516) (primary text to confirm); CCC 673</td></tr>
<tr id="s26"><td><strong>26</strong></td><td><strong>No messianic hope fulfilled within history</strong></td><td>Does it avoid promising an earthly golden age, religious or political, before the end?</td><td>CCC 676; DS 3839</td></tr>

<tr id="s28"><td><strong>28</strong></td><td><strong>The Jewish people justly treated</strong></td><td>Does it avoid blaming or stereotyping the Jewish people, and hold to the Church's hope for Israel?</td><td><em>Nostra Aetate</em> 4; CCC 597, 674</td></tr>
<tr id="s29"><td><strong>29</strong></td><td><strong>Other views stated at their best</strong></td><td>Does it give opposing readings as their holders would, before judging them?</td><td>Ignatius, <em>Spiritual Exercises</em> 22; charter, rules 1 and 4</td></tr>
<tr id="s30"><td><strong>30</strong></td><td><strong>Confidence graded</strong></td><td>Does it grade each claim (possible, probable, settled) and never call settled what the Church has not settled?</td><td>Charter (Oct 19, 2026)</td></tr>
