The Standards

Thirty questions every reading of Revelation 13 is asked, beginning with the Church's own criteria for reading Scripture.

Home › The Standards

✓ Reviewed by Fr. John How this site is made

A reading of Revelation 13 is weighed here in two ways. First, against the marks: what Scripture and the Church reveal about the beasts, set out in depth on The First Beast. Second, against these standards: how a reading uses the marks. The first three are the Church's own criteria for interpreting Scripture (CCC 112–114); the rest come from the Church's documents, the Fathers, Scripture's own tests, and the charter of The Ephesus Challenge.

A standard is a question asked of an argument. Meeting it does not make a reading true, and failing one does not make it false; it shows where the reading is strong and where it needs more support. Every proposal is asked the same questions, Fr. John's included.

The first twelve

#StandardThe question it asks of a proposalBasis
1Unity of ScriptureDoes it read the passage within the whole of Scripture, not in isolation?CCC 112; Dei Verbum 12
2Living TraditionDoes it engage how the Fathers and the Church have read the passage?CCC 113
3Analogy of faithIs it consistent with the truths of the faith as a whole?CCC 114
4Text in its contextDoes each verse it uses say what the proposal needs, in its own context? For example, 1 Thess 5:3 concerns the sudden Day of the Lord.CCC 109–110
5No date-settingDoes it avoid fixing the day or the hour?Matt 24:36; CCC 673
6Private revelation in its placeDoes it treat apparitions and private revelations as aids, never as additions to the faith?CCC 67
7Checkable evidenceDoes each mark it claims rest on evidence a reader can verify?Charter, rule 5
8ConsistencyHas it held steady, or shifted with circumstances or politics?Charter safeguard
9HumilityDoes it claim no more than its evidence allows?2 Pet 1:20; Augustine's reserve on 2 Thess 2 (City of God XX.19)
10Charity and justiceDoes it treat persons justly and avoid stirring hostility?Charter, rules 1 and 4
11Agenda or sincere searchIs its method sound? Relevant context is stated with sources; any judgment of motive is marked as Fr. John's assessment.Charter safeguard
12FruitDoes it lead readers to endurance, faith and hope rather than fear?Rev 13:10; Luke 12:7; charter, rule 6

Further standards

Added on October 20, 2026, from the Church's documents on reading Scripture, the Fathers, and Scripture's own tests.

#StandardThe question it asks of a proposalBasis
13Literal sense firstDoes it establish what the text says before building a spiritual or future reading on it, and argue only from the literal sense?CCC 116; Aquinas, ST I q. 1 a. 10 ad 1; Divino Afflante Spiritu 23; Providentissimus Deus 15
14Literary form and the writer's intentionDoes it read apocalyptic images as apocalyptic, asking what the sacred writer meant in his time?Dei Verbum 12; CCC 109–110; Divino Afflante Spiritu 33–38
15First fulfillment, then further fulfillmentDoes it account for what the text meant first (Antiochus, Rome) before applying it to the end?Dei Verbum 12; Verbum Domini 40–41
16Scripture interprets ScriptureDoes it test a reading against clearer passages elsewhere?Augustine, De Doctrina Christiana III.27.38–28.39; Verbum Domini 39
17Consent of the FathersWhere the Fathers agree on a matter of faith, does it follow them? Where they differ, does it say so and leave freedom?Trent, Session 4; Vatican I, Dei Filius ch. 2; Providentissimus Deus 14; Divino Afflante Spiritu 47
18Everywhere, always, by allIs the reading held widely and anciently, or is it new and local?Vincent of Lérins, Commonitorium 2.6
19Levels of authority kept distinctDoes it label each claim as Scripture, Church teaching, common opinion of the Fathers, a theologian's view, private revelation or the proposer's own view?Dei Verbum 10; CCC 67; charter, rule 2; Miceli, The Antichrist, p. 15, against "the presentation of personal speculations as facts found in these prophecies"
20Application without manipulationWhen it applies the text to present events, does it rest on sound exegesis and avoid projecting ideologies onto the text?PBC, Interpretation of the Bible in the Church (1993) I.F, IV.A.3; Verbum Domini 44
21Not a private readingDoes it submit to the Church's judgment and avoid twisting hard passages?2 Pet 1:20–21; 3:16; CCC 119
22Test everythingHas it been tested against Christ come in the flesh, and does it hold what is good?1 Thess 5:19–21; 1 John 4:1–3
23Failed predictions countHas it, or a version of it, predicted things that did not happen?Deut 18:21–22; Miceli, The Antichrist, p. 97: "All chiliastic movements outlive the nonfulfillment of their prophecies"
24No unhealthy curiosityDoes it seek to know the future in a way that replaces trust in Providence?CCC 2115–2116; Arminjon, The End of the Present World, p. 14: reckonings of the end are "idle quests, lacking any purpose other than the satisfaction of vain curiosity"
25No fixed time for the AntichristDoes it avoid announcing when the Antichrist will come?Lateran V, Session 11 (1516) (primary text to confirm); CCC 673
26No messianic hope fulfilled within historyDoes it avoid promising an earthly golden age, religious or political, before the end?CCC 676; DS 3839
27Builds up charityDoes it build up love of God and neighbor?Augustine, De Doctrina Christiana I.36.40
28The Jewish people justly treatedDoes it avoid blaming or stereotyping the Jewish people, and hold to the Church's hope for Israel?Nostra Aetate 4; CCC 597, 674
29Other views stated at their bestDoes it give opposing readings as their holders would, before judging them?Ignatius, Spiritual Exercises 22; charter, rules 1 and 4
30Confidence gradedDoes it grade each claim (possible, probable, settled) and never call settled what the Church has not settled?Charter (Oct 19, 2026)
