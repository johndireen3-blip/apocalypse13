Mark 9 weighed

Deception by Signs

Great signs, even fire from heaven, done in the first beast's sight to deceive: what each reading points to, and whether it shows a deceiving wonder or an act of state.

The mark
The second beast performs great signs, even making fire come down from heaven in the sight of everyone, and deceives the inhabitants of the earth with signs done in the sight of the first beast (Revelation 13:13–14; 19:20; Deuteronomy 13:2–4; 2 Thessalonians 2:9–10).
What the Church teaches
The Church teaches that the miracles of Christ and the saints are signs that support faith (CCC 156, 548), and that every attempt to tame occult powers is gravely contrary to the virtue of religion (CCC 2117). The final trial is a religious deception (CCC 675): signs that seem to come from heaven, used to win the world to a man. Church teaching

Mark 9 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

Read in context (Standard 4) and with Scripture's own test of signs (Standard 16; Deuteronomy 13:2–4), the mark is a wonder: something that seems to come from heaven, done publicly by the false prophet, which deceives people and leads them toward the first beast and its image. Power, war and spectacle are common to every age and are not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show a sign worked or presented by the one it names, and its deceiving purpose.

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John places the false prophet at the threshold of a war that came "from heaven" in the sight of the world, and the sequence he documents, talks in Geneva and strikes two days later, is accurate. But the mark is a deceiving wonder, and a military strike ordered by a head of state is not shown to be the sign of the one who negotiated before it; nor does it lead anyone toward worship of the first beast. As argued, the evidence places the man near great events, not yet working signs. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as the false prophet's lying wonders at the end. Thiel's reading does not take up this mark.

Arminjon gives the fire from heaven to the first beast, so his reading cannot help decide which figure works the signs.

Miceli gives the fire from heaven to the second beast, as the text does, and allows in his own voice that the signs may be worked by "manipulating the forces of science" (p. 120), which leaves room for signs worked through technology.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
