The mark
The dragon hands the beast his power, throne and great authority, and the world worships the dragon and the beast together (Revelation 13:2, 4).
What the Church teaches
Satan is powerful, but only a creature; his action is permitted by divine providence and cannot stop the building up of God's reign (CCC 395). Christ's victory over the prince of this world was won once for all (CCC 2853). Church teaching
Authority
Scripture
In depth
Mark 1 on The First Beast: the text, the Fathers, the Church, and where interpreters differ

What the mark requires

Read in its context (Standard 4), mark 1 is not great power as such. St. Paul teaches that the authorities that exist are established by God (Romans 13:1). The mark is authority received from the dragon and issuing in the dragon's worship (Revelation 13:4). A reading that claims it must show that link, not only a powerful figure.

The seven readings

The first-century reading (NABRE notes) Yes, of Rome "Satan… commissioned the beast to persecute the church" (note on 13:2); the beast "symbolizes the Roman empire" Applied to the first century only; the notes do not say whether a further fulfillment is to come 13, 14, 15 met

Peter Thiel Not directly Power rises from fear of catastrophe and the promise of "peace and safety" (1 Thessalonians 5:3), offered by a world state (Hoover Institution, 2024); his second essay calls "Peace and safety" the Antichrist's slogan (First Things, August 2026) Neither essay takes up the dragon's gift or the worship that follows; 1 Thessalonians 5:3 concerns the sudden Day of the Lord, not the beast's rise 4 partly met; 1 partly met
Fr. John Yes Satan cannot become incarnate, so he gives his power to a man; deception, the devil's chief work (John 8:44), is shown in a documented pattern of false and suggestive public speech. In November 2026 he lowered his grade from probable to possible, because it "rests on the pattern of deception in his public speech alone," and until the gift of the throne is shown by worship the mark stands "to assess" (his case) Holding power is not itself the mark; deception shows a likeness to the "many antichrists" (1 John 2:18), not by itself the dragon's gift of his throne, which the text ties to worship 2, 3 met on the premise; 7 met for the evidence given; 4 partly met; 9 met: the grade was lowered to what the evidence shows; 30: graded possible, to assess

Verdict on the arguments

St. Irenaeus's reading is strong as exegesis, and it makes no application to the present that could be tested. Fr. John's premise, that Satan works through a man rather than becoming one, matches the Fathers and St. Thomas Aquinas. His evidence is documented and checkable, but it shows deception, which the text does not make the mark itself; the link from the dragon's gift to worship is still to be shown, and that is the test of mark 6. He now says this himself: he has lowered the grade from probable to possible and lists the mark's standing as "to assess." That matches the evidence. Thiel's reading, as far as the material reviewed shows, does not claim this mark.

Fr. Charles Arminjon, like St. Irenaeus, makes Satan the source of the one man's power without taking up the gift of the throne, and applies the mark to no one.

Fr. Vincent Miceli reads the dragon's gift plainly and, like St. Irenaeus, applies it to no one.

Newman, reporting the Fathers, makes the Antichrist the rival of all that is called God, and applies the mark to no one.

The first-century reading gives the mark a sufficient meaning in John's own day: Satan working through Rome. Any reading for our time is a further fulfillment and must show more than Rome already shows.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person. This site never says that anyone is or is not the Antichrist.
