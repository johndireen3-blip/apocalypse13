The mark
The beast is granted authority over every tribe, people, tongue and nation, and all the inhabitants of the earth worship it, except those written in the Lamb's book of life; ten kings give it their power and authority (Revelation 13:7–8; 17:12–18; Daniel 7:23; Luke 4:5–8).
What the Church teaches
The Church does not condemn the unity of nations. Popes have called for a true world political authority to serve the common good, bound by law and by the moral order (St. John XXIII, Pacem in Terris 137; Benedict XVI, Caritas in Veritate 67). What she condemns is power that recognizes nothing above itself: societies that reject God's vision of man "arrogate to themselves an explicit or implicit totalitarian power over man and his destiny" (CCC 2244). The beast's reach is not wrong for being wide but for being given by the dragon and turned to worship. Church teaching

Mark 8 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

Read in context (Standard 4), the beast's reach is authority, given by the dragon and joined to worship (Revelation 13:4, 8), and in Revelation 17 the kings hand their power to it (17:13). Great powers have reached across the world in every age, and the Church herself calls for a world authority bound by law and the moral order (Caritas in Veritate 67), so wide reach is not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show nations giving up their power to the one it names, or a single authority over them centered on him.

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John shows a presidency using its reach over other nations unusually directly, and in his own name: running another country, pressing allies for their land, and chairing a new body of nations. His evidence is documented and his defense is stated. But what it shows is the influence of a great power, contested by most of the world and checked by rivals and allies, not yet the authority over every nation, freely handed over by kings, that the text describes. The Board of Peace is the item that bears most directly on Revelation 17:13 and is worth watching. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully of the one to come. Thiel's world state takes this mark as its center, but his reading identifies a tendency rather than a present power.

Arminjon reads the reach as literal world rule, and notes the means his own century had made possible without naming any state.

Miceli reads the reach as world power seized by one man, with no application to test.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
