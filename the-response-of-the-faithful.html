Mark 14 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

This mark is not claimed; it is lived. Revelation 13 is written so that the holy ones will hear, endure and keep faith, and will not answer the beast with its own weapons (13:10; Matthew 26:52). So every reading is weighed here by its fruit (Standard 12) and by whether it builds up charity (Standard 27): what does it ask of its readers, and does it leave them more faithful, more patient and more loving, or more fearful, curious and hostile?

The four readings

St. Irenaeus Endurance through a trial the Church must pass through, and hope in the Lord's coming and the kingdom of the just; patience rather than speculation about the name (Against Heresies V.30) Little: his book was written to strengthen the faithful against error 12, 27 met

Charles Arminjon (1881) Prepare now, "by having recourse to Him through prayer, and by providing ourselves with the spiritual weapons of charity and faith" (The End of the Present World, p. 48), for "evils foreseen do less harm" (p. 50) His pages on the Jews of his own day (pp. 60–63) praise them but also cast them as a people bent on gold and power, which does not build up charity 12 met; 28 not met on pp. 60–63
Peter Thiel Vigilance against fear: not to surrender freedom, science and technology to a world authority in exchange for "peace and safety" His response is chiefly political and technological, not the endurance and faith the text asks; his public naming of living people as examples of the Antichrist's program (Greta Thunberg, called by him a servant of the Antichrist, and Eliezer Yudkowsky) does not build up charity (CTV News; Gizmodo) 27 not met in the material reviewed; 12 to watch
Fr. John Hear, endure, keep faith, refuse the mark, do not take up the sword, love even now (those who follow the man named, and those who disagree), and pray for all in authority, and do not be afraid; with pages on preparing, discerning, the martyrs and love of enemies (his case) Naming a living, sitting leader as the beast carries a real risk of fear and division among readers, whatever safeguards are written; whether the reading bears good fruit can only be seen over time 12 to watch: fruit is shown over time; 27 met in what it asks; 10 met: words and deeds weighed, never souls; 20 to watch: application without manipulation

Verdict on the arguments

On this mark Fr. John's reading asks of its readers what Revelation 13:9–10 asks: hearing, endurance, faith, refusal of violence and love for those on every side, and it says plainly that the reading fails if it produces fear or hostility. That is the right test, and he sets it for himself. Whether his reading meets it cannot be shown on a page; it will be shown in its readers. St. Irenaeus remains the model: a reading of the last enemy written to make the Church patient and hopeful. Thiel's reading asks for vigilance against fear, but its response is political, and its naming of living people as figures of the Antichrist's program does not build up charity.

Arminjon asks for prayer, charity and faith now; his pages on the Jews of his own day are weighed under Standard 28, as noted on Beyond Revelation 13.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
