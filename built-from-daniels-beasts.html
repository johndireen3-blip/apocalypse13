The mark
A beast from the sea with ten horns and seven heads, like a leopard, a bear and a lion: Daniel's four kingdoms gathered into one (Revelation 13:1–2; Daniel 7:3–8). Revelation explains the heads as seven hills and seven kings (Revelation 17:9–11).

In depth
Mark 2 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

A reading that claims mark 2 must read the beast as Scripture explains it: a power over nations (Daniel 7:17), first fulfilled in Rome (Standard 15), with its heads and horns read as Revelation itself explains them (Revelation 17:9–12; Standard 16).

The three readings

Reading Claims mark 2? Evidence it gives What weighs against Standards
St. Irenaeus Yes The fourth kingdom (Rome) divides among ten kings; the one to come rises among them and subdues three (Against Heresies V.25.3; V.26.1) The "ten kings" have been matched to many periods; nothing in the reading says when they will be seen 1, 2, 15, 16 met
Peter Thiel Partly His Antichrist is a world state or system that wins one-world rule; he has suggested the United States may be both restrainer and possible Antichrist (Hoover Institution, 2024) No account of the heads, horns or first fulfillment in the material reviewed so far 15, 16 not met as far as reviewed
Fr. John Yes, through the heads Seven hills as seven world powers, counted from John's day: Egypt, Assyria, Babylon, Persia and Greece fallen; Rome the one that is; the United States the one to come. The seventh head is his first term ("a short while"), the "was and is not" is 2021–25, and the eighth, which "belongs to the seven," is the 47th presidency (Revelation 17:8–11; his case) The seven-empires count is chiefly a Protestant reading; Catholic commentary, including the NABRE, reads the heads as Roman emperors. There is an eleven-century gap between Rome and the United States, answered by a test (the leading world power of its age, holding the nations in subjection) that rests on a judgment the British Empire might also claim. Grover Cleveland also served two non-consecutive terms 15 met (Rome as the sixth); 16 met on 17:8–11; 18 not met: the count is not the ancient common reading; 7 partly met; 30: graded probable

Verdict on the arguments

St. Irenaeus meets every standard that applies. Thiel's world-state reading fits Daniel's sense of a kingdom, but does not engage the text's details. Fr. John's reading gives a full count anchored in John's own day, and its account of the seventh and eighth heads follows Revelation 17:8–11 closely. It rests on a minority count of the heads, an eleven-century gap, and a test for what qualifies as a head that depends on judgment. Its answer to the Cleveland comparison depends on mark 3.
