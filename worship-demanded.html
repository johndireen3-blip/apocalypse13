Worship Demanded

The whole earth bows to the beast, and a second beast enforces it: what each reading points to, and whether it reaches worship or only loyalty.

The mark
The world worships the dragon and the beast, saying, "Who can compare with the beast or who can fight against it?" The second beast makes the earth worship the first, and has an image made that speaks and has those who will not worship it put to death (Revelation 13:4, 8, 12, 14–15; Daniel 3:5–6).

Mark 6 on The First Beast

What the mark requires

The mark moves from fascination, to worship given, to worship enforced (Revelation 13:3–4, 12, 15). Read in context (Standard 4) and with Daniel 3 (Standard 16), the worship is religious: honor that belongs to God given to a creature (CCC 2096, 2113), required on pain of death. Loyalty to a ruler, devotion to a party and a ruler's name on public works are found in many ages and are not yet the mark. A reading that claims it must show honor due to God being demanded or accepted, and the beginnings of enforcement. It must also say who enforces it, because in the text that is the second beast's work.

Verdict on the arguments

Fr. John documents an unusual concentration of personal honor in a sitting president, loyalty asked of persons, and a consistent price paid by those who refuse it, including former allies, with sources and with the defense on the record. Historians note that presidents have generally avoided such honors while in office. What his evidence shows so far is honor, loyalty and political penalty, not the worship the text describes; the penalties fall on opposition, not on refusal to worship, and none approaches the death sentence of Revelation 13:15. His reading needs the second beast to complete this mark, which is why he grades it only partly seen. St. Irenaeus reads the mark faithfully as divine worship demanded at the end. Thiel's reading takes up the world's submission but not its worship.

Arminjon reads the mark as compelled adoration of one man, as St. Irenaeus does, but without the second beast who enforces it.

These are verdicts on arguments, not on any person.
